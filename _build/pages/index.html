What I work with

The right container matters as much as the right lane.

Liquids don’t ship like boxes. Volume, product type, temperature and what happens at the receiving end all shape whether a move belongs in a flexitank, an ISO tank or a run of IBCs.

How I think about bulk liquids

Flexitanks

A single-use bladder that turns a standard 20’ container into a bulk liquid carrier, for non-hazardous products moving in volume.

ISO tanks

Reusable steel tanks in a container frame, for chemicals, food-grade liquids and products that need heating or tighter handling.

IBCs & totes

Roughly 1,000-litre units for smaller volumes, mixed orders and products that go straight into a customer’s process.

Ocean freight

Where most international bulk liquid volume moves, in full containers by flexitank, ISO tank or IBCs.

Air freight

For urgent, smaller-volume or high-value product, like samples, specialty liquids or anything that can’t wait for a vessel.

How I work

Understand the business first. Then build the move.

A good quote starts with knowing what the product is, where it’s going and what the customer on the other end needs. From there it’s coordination, and staying with it until the product lands.

Talk through a shipment

  1. Learn the product and the customer

    Safety data, grade, volumes, cadence, and how the receiving site actually unloads.

  2. Match the equipment and the mode

    Flexitank, ISO tank or IBCs; ocean, air or a combination.

  3. Coordinate the lane

    Pricing, operations, carriers, overseas offices and partners, so the quote holds up in execution.

  4. Stay with the shipment

    Supporting execution and communication from booking to delivery.

  5. Grow from there

    Looking for the next lane, product or market where the account can expand.
